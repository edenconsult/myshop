# 01. Sign in, and your own account

## What this is for

This is the door to the shop, and the only screen everyone who works here has to know. You come to it
with the login name and password the owner handed you, and you leave it standing on your own home
screen. Between those two moments you can also fix the two things the shop prints against your name —
the names you go by and the password you use — without touching anybody else's account.

## You need

- **Who can open it:** anybody with an account, at any role. There is no owner-only version of this
  screen and no cashier-only version; the sign-in card is the front door for the whole building.
- **Where it lives:** it is what the browser shows first. Open the shop address the owner gave you and
  you are on the card without typing anything else. If you are already signed in on that browser, the
  same address does not show you the card again — it sends you straight to your own home screen, the
  dashboard for an owner and the till for a cashier.
- **How to reach it again from inside the shop:** the red `Log Out` button in the header, on both the
  desktop bar and the phone drawer. It is the only way to get back here on purpose.
- **What must already exist:** an account, made by the owner on the accounts screen. Nothing on this
  page creates one, repairs one, or emails one.
- **What to have ready:** your two credentials and nothing else. If you get them wrong, the username is
  kept in its box and the password is cleared, so you only ever retype the password.
- **What you cannot get from this screen:** a password hint, a recovery address, or a second factor. If
  the password is genuinely gone, the owner has to set a new one for you.
- **What to check before you type anything:** the name in the header of any screen you were already on.
  If you were mid-task and have landed here instead, it is your session that ended, not your password
  that changed — see "When it goes wrong".

## Steps

### 1. Type the two names of your account and press Log In

![The sign-in card: on the left, the Noble Cosmetics mark with the line "Store Central Hub" under it, the heading "Sign in to Store #01", a short sentence about ringing up customer sales, keeping the stock ledger accurate and reviewing the day's takings, and three dots reading "Point of sale", "Products & stock", "Orders & reports". On the right, a "USERNAME" box showing the grey placeholder "Enter Username", a "PASSWORD" box showing "Enter Password", and a full-width green "LOG IN" button. Along the bottom, a "Developed by / Eden Creative Systems / ecreativesys.com" credit with an email address and a phone number.](img/01-sign-in/01-login.png)

The username box is already selected when the page opens, so you can type into it immediately and press
Tab to reach the password. The three dots are decoration — nothing behind them. The developer credit
along the bottom is not decoration: it is three live links out of the shop, to the maker's site, to an
email address and to a phone number, and pressing one leaves the shop. None of them is part of your work
here, and nothing you press there can reach your account.

### 2. If the credentials are wrong, read the red band and try again

![The same card with a pale red band across the top of the right-hand column reading "Username or password is incorrect!". The username box now holds a typed name, the password box is empty again showing "Enter Password", and the "LOG IN" button is unchanged.](img/01-sign-in/01-login-error.png)

Your username is kept for you; the password box is cleared. The band does not say which of the two was
wrong, and it is not a lock-out — you may try again as many times as you like, and nothing is recorded
against your account for failing.

### 3. Once you are in, find the account badge in the header

There is no separate frame for this: the signed-in screens carry a round badge at the top right showing
your own initials and, beside it, a name. That name is your profile's first and last name when they are
filled in, and your login name when they are not — which is why the profile frame in step 4 reads
`tutorial` with all four boxes empty. The badge is the shop's only proof of who is holding the keyboard.
Press it and the menu that opens lists `Your profile`, `Change password`, and — for an owner only —
`Manage accounts`. What prints on a receipt is a different thing again: that line uses your two names in
the other order, last name first, and chapter 09 covers it.

### 4. Open your own details and fill in what the shop should print

![A signed-in screen with the full header: the Noble Cosmetics mark, then "Central Hub", "Point of Sale", "Products & Stock", "Orders", "Sales Reports", "Help", then the account badge showing the signed-in person's name with a small green dot, a dark-mode button, and a red "Log Out" button. Below it, a card headed "Your profile" with the sentence "These are the details shown against the sales you record. Your username and role are handled by an administrator." and four labelled boxes: "FIRST NAME", "LAST NAME", "EMAIL", "PHONE", with the note "Up to 10 digits." under the phone box and a green "Save changes" button beneath.](img/01-sign-in/01-profile.png)

Everything in this card is yours to edit. Your login name and your role are deliberately not in it — the
card says so in its own words. Fill at least the two names. With them blank the shop falls back to your
login name on the hub, in the header badge and in the reports, but a printed receipt is not one of the
places that falls back: its `Served by:` line just prints blank, which is why the names matter.

### 5. Save it and take the confirmation

Nothing new to photograph: pressing `Save changes` reloads the same card with a green note reading
`Profile updated.` above the form. If the note does not appear, the save did not happen — look for a red
line under whichever box was refused.

### 6. Change your password from the same menu

![A card headed "Change password" with the sentence "Confirm the password you use today, then choose the new one. You stay signed in on this device after the change." Below it three required boxes marked with red asterisks: "CURRENT PASSWORD *", "NEW PASSWORD *", "CONFIRM NEW PASSWORD *". Under the new-password box, four plain lines of guidance: "Your password can't be too similar to your other personal information.", "Your password must contain at least 8 characters.", "Your password can't be a commonly used password.", "Your password can't be entirely numeric." At the bottom a green "Change password" button and a "Back to profile" link beside it.](img/01-sign-in/01-password-change.png)

Those four lines are the rules your new password has to satisfy; they are the whole test, not advice.
Type the password you use today in the first box or the change is refused. `Back to profile` leaves
without saving.

### 7. Leave the confirmation page and get on with the day

![A card with a green tick badge beside the heading "Password changed" and the sentence "Use the new password next time you sign in. Any other device still needs the old one, so sign out there if it matters." Under it a green "Back to profile" button and a plain "Continue" button with a hub icon.](img/01-sign-in/01-password-done.png)

`Continue` takes you back to your own home screen — the dashboard for an owner, the till for a cashier.
You are not signed out by changing your password.

Do not read this frame as proof that a password was changed. It is the page you land on after a
successful change, and it is also a page any signed-in browser can open directly, with nothing written.
That is how it was photographed for this book, so no password was touched to make it — the same rule the
whole manual follows. The sentence printed on the card is wrong about other devices, and the last bullet
under "When it goes wrong" puts that right.

## When it goes wrong

- **You press Log In with nothing in the username box.** A small red line appears under that box:
  `Username is empty`. It is a form check, not a wrong password, and the password box is untouched.
- **You press Log In with nothing in the password box.** The same style of line, under the password:
  `Password is empty`. Both lines appear together if both boxes were left blank.
- **The name exists but the password is wrong, or the name does not exist at all.** Either way you get
  the band from step 2: `Username or password is incorrect!`. The screen will not tell you which one was
  wrong, so a typo in the username and a stale password look identical here.
- **A deactivated account is refused in the same words.** Someone the owner has switched off gets
  `Username or password is incorrect!` even when both entries are correct. There is no "this account is
  deactivated" message on this screen; if you are certain the password is right, ask the owner to look at
  the accounts list.
- **You are bounced here without pressing anything, because your session ended.** This is the one thing
  a new cashier should not panic about. A sign-in that has quietly run out sends you back to this exact
  card. Nothing on it says which happened, and there is no "you are not allowed" note anywhere — the shop
  forgets where you were going. Sign in again and you will be sent to your own home screen, not to the
  screen you were reaching for, so walk there yourself from the header.
- **You are not bounced here for opening a screen your role is not allowed to see.** That is the other
  bounce, and it never draws this card. A cashier who is properly signed in and reaches an owner-only
  screen is turned around at the door without being shown it, and the shop sends them straight back to
  their own register. No door, no note, no reason given: you press something and land on your own screen.
  That is a permissions answer, not a crash. Walk back to what you were doing from the header, or ask the
  owner. This card only appears when the browser is genuinely signed out.
- **The login card appears inside the middle of a screen you were already using.** If your session ends
  while a page is quietly loading a table, a panel or a dialog underneath itself — a filter, a page of
  results, a progress box — the sign-in card can swap into that area instead of replacing the whole
  page. You will see the header and your half-filled screen with a login box where the rows should be.
  That is not a broken filter. Sign in again in that box and the whole page comes back, rows and all.
- **Your profile refuses a long phone number.** The phone box will not take an eleventh character: it
  stops you while you type and cuts a long paste short, so you cannot get an over-length number into it
  from the keyboard at all. The note under it says `Up to 10 digits.` The red line that same limit would
  produce is real but you will not reach it by typing — it only comes back if something posts a longer
  value around the box.
- **A new password is refused.** The change form answers with a red line under the box that caused it: a
  wrong current password, a new password that breaks one of the four rules in step 6, or the two new
  boxes not matching. Nothing changes until all three are satisfied.
- **You change your password and then find the till on the other computer wants you to sign in again.**
  The confirmation screen says the opposite in its own words — `Any other device still needs the old one,
  so sign out there if it matters.` — but that line is words printed on a page, not what the shop does.
  Changing a password signs out every other session for that account, and the other till stops working on
  its very next press. It needs the new password. There is nothing to sign out by hand, and no way to
  leave it running.

## Notes & limits

- **There is no "forgot password" anything on this screen.** No link, no box, no email. The shop is not
  configured to send email at all, on any screen, for any reason.
- **This screen cannot make an account, rename one, or change a role.** Making and renaming accounts
  belong to the owner, on the accounts screen. Changing a role belongs to nobody: in this build every
  account the owner makes signs in as a cashier, and the accounts screen says so in its own words —
  `New accounts sign in as salespersons; roles are not chosen here.`
- **Your profile cannot change your login name or your role**, and the card says so:
  `Your username and role are handled by an administrator.`
- **"Up to 10 digits" counts length, not digits.** The box will hold ten characters of anything; it is
  not a phone validator. Type the number without spaces or a plus sign and it will fit.
- **The profile card shows no email confirmation and no password box.** Email here is a detail on file,
  nothing more — it is never used to reach you, and it is not a login name.
- **Your own role is written out in exactly one place:** the phone drawer, under your name in the account
  strip at its foot. The profile card and the desktop menu never name it. Other people's roles are
  printed too, in a `Role` column on the accounts screen — that is where the owner reads who is what.
- **The password box has no reveal switch,** on the sign-in card or on the change form, and there is no
  "remember me" and no second factor. What you type is what is checked.
- **The shop sets no idle timeout of its own.** It says nothing about sessions at all, which leaves
  Django's default two-week sign-in in place: a browser stays signed in for about a fortnight unless you
  log out first. That is why a shared counter machine should always be signed out at the end of a shift.
- **The dark-mode button and the theme you choose belong to the machine, not to your account.** Sign in
  somewhere else and it starts from the shop default again.
- **The sign-in card carries no status panel.** There is no "is the shop even up?" self-check on it, and
  no mention of the background service. If the browser cannot reach the address at all, the problem is
  the shop's start-up rather than your password, and that is an upkeep question — chapter 13 and the
  shop's own setup notes cover it.
- **What the other role sees at the same addresses:** after signing in, an owner's home is the dashboard
  in chapter 02; a cashier's home is the till in chapter 08, at the very same address. A cashier's header
  also drops four of the six menu items — they get Point of Sale and Help only — and their account menu
  has no "Manage accounts".
- **This screen never shows money, products or sales.** The first number you meet is on your own home
  screen.
